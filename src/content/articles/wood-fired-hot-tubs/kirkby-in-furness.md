A wood-fired hot tub is heated by a stainless steel stove instead of an electric heater. For a home or a holiday let in Kirkby-in-Furness, that changes three things: how fast the water gets hot, what electrical supply you need, and how you keep it at the temperature you like. This resource covers all three, then how the tubs are built and what happens when you order one.

## How fast a wood-fired hot tub heats up

From cold, a wood-fired tub reaches around 38°C in about 2 to 4 hours. A 2-metre family tub takes about two hours even in hard winter. An electric hot tub, by comparison, takes 14 to 16 hours to heat from cold.

That speed is the main reason people choose wood. You can decide in the afternoon that you want the tub tonight, light the stove, and be in it by the evening. There is no need to keep the water at temperature all week in case you want to use it.

One thing to know before you start: the water keeps climbing for as long as you keep adding wood. Once it is near 35°C, stop adding wood and let the stove carry it the rest of the way.

## Keeping the water at the temperature you want

The commonest worry about wood-fired hot tubs is control. With an electric tub you set a number; with a stove, people expect it to be guesswork. It is not.

- **The vent.** The stove has an open and closed vent. Open it to draw heat; shut it to slow the burn down.
- **The thermometer.** A digital thermometer on the outside of the tub shows the exact water temperature, so you always know where it is.
- **Overnight.** Stop adding wood and the temperature does not crash. The water is still warm in the morning.

It also gives you more range than an electric tub. Standard electric tubs are governed to 40°C. Body temperature is 36.7°C; most people like about 38°C in autumn and about 40°C in hard winter, and with a stove you choose.

## Jets, LED lights and a 13-amp socket

Most buyers choose the model with 10 to 12 hydrotherapy jets and a couple of LED lights. Because the stove does the heating, a standard 13-amp socket runs only the jets and the lights. No special electrical supply is needed.

Buyers choose where the water and air massage jets go and how strong they are. Filtration is by a sand filter and a UV lamp.

A completely off-grid starter model is also made, with no electricity at all. It is popular with lodge parks and glamping sites, but it is a small share of what sells: most people want the jets and the lights.

The reasons owners give for choosing wood stay the same:

- renewable wood heat, and less reliance on electricity;
- off-grid use where there is no supply;
- the traditional bathing experience, with the natural aroma of burning wood;
- lower running costs if you have your own wood.
